## CHAPTER 1
It is raining heavily. You are walking along a soaking gravel path, leading to the entrance to the airport of Faroe.

“Weather isn’t looking so great”, you think to yourself, walking faster and faster to reach the airport quicker.

 You booked a flight couple of weeks in advance already. And just now, you get a notification on your phone. “Widerøe: Your flight WF285 to Bergen is delayed. Due to weather, this flight will now be operated by Norwegian (Boeing 737-800).”. “What a shocker”- you said, continuing walking down a wet gravel path.

Suddenly the rain stops, and you experience a slightly warmer wind, that lifts your hopes and gives you more desire to complete the trip, as you have set out to do. However, the sun didn’t last for long, and in the next 10 minutes, it started raining yet again.

Luckily for you, you are already close to the main terminal, and aren’t affected much by the tremendous amount of water that just fell from the sky. “Well that was lucky”, you mumbled.


“Widerøe flight 285 to Bergen, operated by Norwegian, is now boarding at Gate 2,” came loudly over the terminal intercom. “Here we go,” you said to yourself, heading toward the gate.


Unlike on any flights before, you had a strange feeling. You couldn’t describe it, it wasn’t something you knew what it was. There was one thing clear however. From now on, there is no turning back.


### PASSAGE 1.1

The large B737 had just made a sharp turn onto the runway to backtrack and prepare for the takeoff. The engines spooled up loudly as the plane began accelerating to a high-speed taxi. The severe weather and flooded runway were shaking the plane heavily.

Next to you, sat an elderly man. Clearly, he wasn’t so excited about the flight. His face was trembling with an expression you’ve never seen before.

(CHOICE A: HELP THE MAN)

You know what it feels like to drown in panic. Reaching out to him, you tap him on the shoulder. He jumps, turning his eyes full of fear to you. “It’s just some light precipitation”, you said, hopefully easing the mans nerves. “Pilots deal with this stuff every day. Even better, we are flying in a much more reliable aircraft now.”. The man nodded, and turned his face forwards. The fear was slowly draining from his expression.

(CHOICE B: IGNORE)

Despite his expression, you look away, turning your face to a window which was really blurry, due to all the rain. You have enough of your own problems on your shoulders to carry tonight. You cannot afford to take another man’s problems on yourself. You throw the headphones onto your head and turn on some music, to silence any cries of the passengers.

### PASSAGE 1.2
 
“Nordic 248, Faroe tower, Wind 280 degrees at 20 knots, gusting to 28 kts, fly runway heading, runway 30 cleared for takeoff”, was slightly audible from the cockpit. The same moment, the 2 CFM engines roared to life, and the plane started accelerating rapidly down a flooded runway. 

As the wings gained authority, the whole plane was jolted to the side with the sudden gust of crosswind, and for a moment, it appeared as if the engine cowling had actually striked the asphalt underneath. No sparks, no fire, just the cowling was ever so slightly deformed. 

Despite the incident, the pilots continued the takeoff, as if nothing had happened. Presumably, they haven’t even noticed the impact.

(CHOICE A: REPORT THE DAMAGE)

As soon as the plane was in a level flight and had gained some altitude, you swiftly unbuckled your seatbelt, and sprung to the front galley to report the damage to the flight attendants. 
“On the takeoff roll, the right engine cowling had struck the asphalt, and appeared slightly deformed”, you said to the flight attendants. “Thanks for reporting, we will report that to the captain. Now return to your seat and make sure your seatbelt is fastened”. You went back to your seat, with your mind slightly eased. 

(CHOICE B: KEEP QUIET)

You instantly imagined all of the dangerous scenarios that could have happened. Your mind was filled with terrifying thoughts, and wasn’t giving you the peace of mind that you should’ve had on this short hop from Faroe to Bergen. The slight vibration that the engine was now causing wasn’t particularly of your liking.

Plane continued climbing loudly through the clouds for another 30 to 40 minutes. It was hard to tell because you had absolutely zero visual clue. 

## CHAPTER 2 (LOST IN STORM)

The plane had seemingly leveled off, after a long and turbulent climb. However, you could barely see outside, due to the stormy weather. “The pilots must know what they’re doing in order to fly through here”, you thought for yourself. Despite it being a relatively short flight, you’ve drank and absorbed way too much water, and needed to go to the bathroom. The light was green, and there was nobody at the door at the moment. You got up from your seat, shuffled sideways, and strolled to the toilet. 

### Passage 2.1

Before you could reach the door however, a sudden gust of wind had hit the airplane at a very precise angle, and had sent everyone flying. To make things worse, it wasn’t just a gust - it was a whole stream of the same air, and the plane shook violently. The vibration from the engine was at the correct frequency, that made the food trolley retainers unscrew. 

The trolley went flying. If nobody would stop it, it would crush the legs of everyone in the aisle seats.

<TIMED 5 SECONDS>
(CHOICE A: Help the flight attendants secure the trolley back)

You lunged forward to reach the trolley before it had picked up any speed. Your fingers came in contact with the handle, and your hand had pulled the handle into your fist. The weight of the trolley overwhelmed you slightly, and dragged you several meters across the aisle. 

The trolley went to a halt. You were half suspended over the ground, arching for a grip of the trolley. 2 flight attendants ran towards you, one from the back and one from the front. 

“That was really brave” , one of them said. All 3 of you then worked together on a solution on how to secure the trolley for the rest of the flight. 

As you return to your seat, the passengers look slightly different on you. Seemingly, with hope. 

(CHOICE B: Ignore)

There simply wasn’t enough time. You saw the cart speed up along the isle, and had 0 chance of reaching it before it had impacted someone.

Sure enough, not even 2 seconds since, a loud audible cry came from the middle of the plane. You swiftly turned your head, and saw the cart tipped over, obstructing the aisle and injuring 2 aisle seating passengers. Not even a second later, boh flight attendants scrambled to help, leaving you alone in the galley, just staring at what had just happened.

### Passage 2.2

The post was now empty, and there was no one to notice the cracking sound from the overhead bins. The jolt from the turbulence had apparently exceeded the maximum G-load of the overhead bins by an amount, and their structural integrity was compromised. You were in the lavatory at the time when the part of the bin detached. The only thing that you’ve heard is the loud bang and a sudden cry of a passenger.

(CHOICE A: Help the passenger)
You sprinted out of the lavatory, and you saw a very scary scene. The overhead bin, loaded with bags, had partly broken off from the ceiling, and now was on the floor, clamping passenger’s arm to the seat, as they cried in agony. 
With the help of 4 other people, you have lifted the bin and pushed it over to the side. The remaining cabin crew  had rushed with the first aid kits to aid the person with their hand injury.
“What a nightmare of a flight this is, wow”, you mumbled to yourself, realising that you will be scarred forever after what you have just witnessed.
